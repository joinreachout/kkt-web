Deep AI capability is necessary, not sufficient. What ships the work is PMO discipline — scope, sequencing, ownership, delivery under constraint — and starting from the business question, not the technology.

Kitty Kat Technologies (KKT) is a senior PMO and AI delivery firm registered in Tallinn, Estonia. The firm works with mid-sized companies — typically $50M to $500M in annual revenue — across Central Europe, the EU, and Central Asia, with the deepest track record in retail and fuel. Every engagement starts with a two-week diagnostic. Delivery follows a three-stage model: strengthen the core, optimize and grow, and — where the client’s maturity supports it — expand and monetize. The exit criterion, that the system runs without KKT, is built in from week one. KKT does not produce strategy decks without a delivery team behind them, and turns down work where the case for shipping is not yet there. Its flagship product, Optimus, is a live operating-intelligence system for fuel networks, running today at Red Petroleum, a 250-station fuel network in Kyrgyzstan.

What we believe.

  1. 01

    Outcomes set the bar.

    AI earns its place by moving margin, cash, availability, customer value, or productivity — or it doesn’t ship. If the case isn’t there, we say so.

  2. 02

    PMO is the rigor.

    Scope, sequencing, ownership, shipping under constraint. Without that discipline, deliverables become decks. With it, systems run.

  3. 03

    Senior counterpart, always.

    We work alongside your team, not over it. A senior internal owner with authority and time is a precondition, not a bonus. If the room is empty when we leave, the project will fail.

  4. 04

    Until the system runs without us.

    The exit criterion is built in from week one. Engagements end; the product keeps operating. Counterparts inherit the system, not the dependency.

How we work

From question to delivered AI/data solution.

We do not start with technology. We start with the business question, check whether AI and data can realistically help, then move through scope, delivery, adoption, and scale.

  1. 01

    Identify & qualify

    We clarify the business question, understand the current situation, and assess whether AI, data, analytics, automation, or process redesign can realistically help.

    • A clear view of the business issue, owner, and expected result
    • First check of available data, process reality, and feasibility
    • A recommendation to proceed, reshape the idea, or stop early
  2. 02

    Scope & prepare

    We turn a promising opportunity into a delivery-ready project that business and technical teams can understand.

    • What needs to change in the process and decision flow
    • What data, systems, people, and rules are needed
    • Delivery plan, requirements, owners, and acceptance criteria
  3. 03

    Execute & coordinate

    We support implementation from the business side and keep technical delivery connected to business outcomes.

    • Coordinated work between business, vendors, and technical teams
    • Regular checks that the solution still solves the business problem
    • Testing, acceptance, stakeholder alignment, and adoption support
  4. 04

    Close, adopt & scale

    We help make the solution operational, measurable, and ready for further improvement.

    • A working solution embedded into the business process
    • Handover, user adoption, and value tracking
    • Clear next steps for scaling or improving the solution
