I spent fifteen years in an industry that finds the gap between procedures and what people actually do. Most companies know this, few say it: their procedures are written to pass an audit, not to run the company. AI just made that impossible to hide.

For decades, everyone quietly accepted the gap. Including auditors.

Then a new kind of auditor arrived, one nobody invited: the AI agent. It can't look the other way. When a step is missing, it fills the gap from the procedures: what should happen, not what actually happens. And it argues so well that management trusts it over its own people.

Through 2025 and 1H 2026, across mid-market projects in retail, logistics and fuel, I thought the hard part was the AI agent: orchestration, guardrails, evals. The business rules were an attachment. In September 2026 I would say that is only part truth.

## The hard part lived between teams

On two recent projects, the hardest part was the same: rules that lived between teams. At a global cargo company, it was pricing: the written floor said one thing, long-standing clients got another, settled deal by deal between finance and the customer team. At a large food retailer, it was purchasing: commercial and supply chain KPIs pulled opposite ways, and buyers often ordered outside the procedure. It worked. Everyone knew. Nobody tracked it.

An agent can't work with "everyone knows". It needs a rule it can execute, an owner it can escalate to, or a point where it stops and asks. And when it asks, no AI can answer. Only the company can.

## The real black box

I've been telling clients this for a while, and the market is agreeing on the same point. BCG puts 70% of AI value in people and processes. IBM says agents are exposing poorly documented workflows. Snowflake and Databricks build semantic layers so agents query the business, not raw tables. But reading the rules isn't the hard part. Resolving them is.

For all the talk about AI being a black box, the real black box is the company.

Your company's first production-grade AI agent will be the most honest audit it ever gets. Before it can run any part of your company, it will force you to decide how the company actually runs. That alone can be worth as much as the AI automation.
